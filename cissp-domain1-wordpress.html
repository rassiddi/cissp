Risk terminology — tap any card to expand
Asset
Tangible or intangible item of value. Valued in monetary ($) or non-monetary terms (time, productivity, R&D). All assets must be identified, valued, and classified before controls can be applied.
Threat
Any potential action or inaction that can cause damage to an asset. Threat Agents intentionally exploit vulnerabilities. Threat Events may be accidental or intentional.
Vulnerability
Weakness in an asset or absence/weakness in a safeguard. Exposure Factor (EF) = percentage of asset lost in an incident. Every event of exposure is a risk.
Risk
Risk = Threat × Vulnerability. Risk = Probability of harm × Severity of harm. Possibility that a threat will exploit a vulnerability. Risk management is primarily the responsibility of upper management.
Exposure
Possibility that a vulnerability can or will be exploited. Threat vectors include: email, web, external drives, WiFi, physical, mobile, cloud, social media, supply chain.
Breach
Security being bypassed or thwarted by a Threat Agent. An Attack is an intentional attempted exploitation of a vulnerability. A breach may or may not result in successful data compromise.
Risk Appetite
Total amount of risk the organisation is willing to accept overall. Risk Capacity = maximum risk the org can absorb. Risk Tolerance = per-individual-asset acceptable amount/level of risk.
Inherent vs Residual Risk
Inherent/Initial Risk = natural native risk prior to any management efforts. Residual Risk (which management chooses to accept) = Total Risk – Controls Gap. Control Risk is introduced by countermeasures.
Risk response strategies
Mitigation / Reduction
Apply a safeguard, control or countermeasure to reduce the risk to an acceptable level.
Assignment / Transfer
Insurance or outsourcing — shift the financial liability of risk to a third party.
Deterrence
Auditing, cameras, banners, guards — discourage attacks before they occur.
Avoidance
Choose an alternate approach entirely — e.g. fly instead of drive to eliminate a specific risk.
Acceptance
Cost of safeguard outweighs the risk. Must be formally documented and signed. Becomes Residual Risk.
⚠ Reject / Ignore
Considered NEGLIGENCE in court. Never an acceptable risk management strategy.

Control types explained
Preventive
Deployed to thwart or stop unwanted/unauthorised activity before it occurs. Removes or reduces a vulnerability. Examples: Separation of duties, Job rotation, DLP, Pen testing, auditing.
Deterrent
Deployed to discourage security policy violations. Does not prevent but discourages. Examples: Guards, cameras, warning banners, disciplinary policies.
Detective
Deployed to discover or detect unwanted/unauthorised activity after it occurs. Examples: Guards, job rotation, mandatory vacations, audit trails, IDS/IPS, SIEM.
Compensating
Alternative control when the primary control is not feasible. Provides an alternate means to satisfy the requirement. Examples: DRP, Backup systems, clipping levels.
Corrective
Modifies environment to return system to normal after an incident. Examples: Backup and restore, reboot, antivirus quarantine, patching.
Recovery
Extension of Corrective controls with more advanced/complex abilities. Examples: Fault-tolerant drives, Hot/warm/cold sites, RAID, full system redundancy.
Directive
Security policies, escape routes, loose attempt monitoring, supervision, guidance from security guard. Directs people in compliant behaviours.
SCA — Security Control Assessment
If a security control's benefit cannot be quantified, evaluated, or compared — it DOES NOT actually provide security.
SCA definition
Formal evaluation of a security infrastructure's individual mechanism against a baseline of reliability expectations. Generally implemented by federal agencies based on NIST 800-53.
SCA Goal 1
Ensure the effectiveness of the security mechanism deployed in the environment.
SCA Goal 2
Evaluate the quality and thoroughness of the Risk Management (RM) process employed.
SCA Goal 3
Produce a report of the relative strength and weaknesses of the deployed security infrastructure.

Key concepts
Mandatory Vacations
Used as a peer review process. Peer uses the SAME ACCOUNT of the person on vacation. Common in the financial industry. Purpose: to detect abuse, fraud or negligence. Forces dependency exposure.
Separation of Duties
No single person controls a critical process end-to-end. Prevents fraud and errors by requiring collusion between multiple parties to subvert controls. Essential in high-risk environments.
Collusion
Several people work together to perpetrate a crime. Countered by separation of duties, job rotation, and cross-training. People are the weakest link in the security chain but the key asset if trained.
UBA / UEBA
User Behaviour Analytics / User & Entity Behaviour Analysis. Analyses the behaviour of users, subjects, visitors, and entities for specific security goals such as insider threat detection.
Principle of Least Privilege
Minimum amount of access necessary for the job. IAM accounts should be provisioned based on Job Description (JD) and responsibilities. Regular review and audit of JD required.
CISO / CSO role
Enforces administrative controls and compliance/policy requirements. Reports to senior management. Responsible for organisation-wide security posture and strategy.

Attack types
Phishing
Deceptive email to steal credentials or install malware
Spear Phishing
Phishing crafted to a specific group or individual

Targets CEO/C-level execs with detailed research and planning. BEC/CEO Fraud

SMS phishing. SPIM = Spam over Instant Messaging

Gathering intelligence from discarded materials

Baiting
Drops infected USB drives hoping someone uses them

Collects and publicly releases info to damage the target's reputation
Influence Campaigns
Hybrid warfare — manipulates public opinion via social media
Drive-by Downloads
Malware installs silently when user visits a compromised website
Email countermeasures
SPF
Sender Policy Framework — verifies that the sending mail server is authorised by the domain's DNS records to send email on its behalf. Prevents domain spoofing.
DKIM
DomainKeys Identified Mail — cryptographic signature in email headers that verifies the email was not altered in transit and came from the stated domain.
DMARC
Domain Message Authentication Reporting and Conformance — builds on SPF and DKIM to filter spoofed messages and generate reports on email authentication failures.
Awareness training
Three levels: 1. Awareness — brings security to forefront. 2. Training — targeted to job function groups. 3. Education — deep learning beyond immediate work needs. Security Champions encourage peers.
ISC² Code of Ethics
Preamble
Safety and welfare of society requires adherence to highest ethical standards. Strict adherence is a condition of CISSP certification. Act honorably, honestly, justly, responsibly, and legally.
Canon 1
Protect Society, the Common Good, necessary public trust and confidence, and the Infrastructure. Promote and preserve public trust. Discourage unsafe practices.
Canon 2
Act honorably, honestly, justly, responsibly and legally. Tell the truth. Observe all contracts and agreements. Give prudent advice — avoid unnecessary alarm or unwarranted comfort.
Canon 3
Provide diligent and competent service to principals. Render only services for which you are fully competent. Respect client trust and privileges granted to you.
Canon 4
Advance and protect the profession. Maintain competence; keep skills and knowledge current. Sponsor those best qualified for professional advancement.
RFC 1087 (Jan 1989)
Defines unethical internet behaviour — Internet Activities Board (IAB): 1. Unauthorised access 2. Misuse of internet 3. Wastes resources 4. Destroys integrity 5. Compromises privacy.
US cyber laws chronology
1984 — CCCA
Comprehensive Crime Control Act
First federal computer crime law. Established basis for computer crime prosecution.
1986 — CFAA
Computer Fraud and Abuse Act
Outlawed malicious code. Covered Federal Interest Computers. Imprisonment regardless of intent.
1991 — Federal Sentencing Guidelines
Prudent Man Rule
Minimise punishment by demonstrating due diligence. 3 burdens of proof for negligence.
1994 — CAAA
Computer Abuse Amendments Act
Expanded CFAA to international commerce. Civil action enabled for victims.
1996 — NIIPA
National Information Infrastructure Protection Act
Extended protections to railroads, gas pipelines, power grids, and telecom circuits.
2002 — FISMA
Federal Information Security Management Act
NIST standards mandated for federal information systems.
2010 — RMF
NIST Risk Management Framework
SP 800-37 established. NIST 800-53 for federal, 800-171 for non-federal.
2014 — CEA / NCPA / FISMA 2014
Cybersecurity Enhancement + FISMA Modernisation
DHS integration centre. NIST CSF (voluntary). Centralised federal cybersecurity responsibility.
Privacy laws chronology
1791 — 4th Amendment
"Every man's house is his castle"
Constitutional foundation of US privacy rights.
1974 — Privacy Act
Federal data disclosure law
Controls FED's ability to disclose information. Written consent required. Formal access process.
1986 — ECPA
Electronic Communications Privacy Act
Crime to invade electronic privacy. $500 and 5 years prison for mobile wiretapping.
1996 — HIPAA
Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act
Protects health information for insurance and health maintenance organisations.
1998 — COPPA / ITADA
Children's Online Privacy + Identity Theft Act
Parental consent for under-13s. Identity theft made a crime — up to 15 years / $250K fine.
1999 — GLBA
Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act
Relaxed barriers between banks, insurance, and credit providers.
2018 / 2020 — GDPR / CCPA
EU GDPR + California Consumer Privacy Act
GDPR in effect 2018. CCPA modelled on GDPR, in effect 2020. Privacy Shield invalidated 2020 (Schrems II).
