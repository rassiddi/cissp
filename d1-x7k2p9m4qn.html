Core risk formulas — must memorise
SLE=AV × EF
ALE=SLE × ARO
Value of Safeguard=(ALE_pre – ALE_post) – ACS
Total Risk=AV × Vulnerability × Threats
Residual Risk=Total Risk – Controls Gap
Risk = Threat × Vulnerability = Probability of harm × Severity of harm. Budget: if not constrained, go for the $$$ (quantitative RA). Not possible to eliminate ALL risk — goal is to get risk to an acceptable/tolerable level. Risk management is primarily the responsibility of UPPER MANAGEMENT.
Risk terminology — tap to expand
Asset
Tangible or intangible item of value. Monetary: $X. Non-monetary: time, attention, productivity, R&D. All assets must be identified, valued, and classified. Asset Valuation → Asset-Threat pairing → Risk Assessment.
Threat
Any potential action/inaction which can cause damage. Threat Agent/Actors: intentionally exploit vulnerabilities. Threat Event: accidental OR intentional exploit. Threat Vector: path taken (email, web, USB, WiFi, physical, mobile, cloud, social media, supply chain).
Vulnerability
Weakness in an asset OR absence/weakness in a safeguard. Exposure Factor (EF) = % of asset value lost. Every event of exposure is a risk. NIST SP 800-30 Rev.1 Appendix D = Threat Sources; Appendix E = Threat Events.
Risk appetite vs tolerance
Risk Appetite: total AMOUNT of risk organisation will accept overall. Risk Capacity: maximum level of risk organisation can absorb. Risk Tolerance: per-individual-asset acceptable amount/level of risk. Inherent Risk = natural native risk prior to any management efforts.
Qualitative vs Quantitative RA
Qualitative: Scenarios, Delphi method — NIST SP 800-30 Rev.1. Subjective scoring. Faster, less expensive. Quantitative: assigns $ values (AV, EF, SLE, ARO, ALE, Cost/Benefit Analysis). Objective but time-consuming. Most orgs use a HYBRID approach. Baselines = minimum standards (ISO 27005).
Risk register / heat map
Risk Register: documents identified risks, severity/priority, response, and tracking progress. Risk Matrix/Heat Map: graph comparing probability vs damage potential. Sometimes labelled as qualitative RA. ERM = Enterprise Risk Management — holistic organisational programme. RMM = Risk Maturity Model (Adhoc → Preliminary → Defined → Integrated → Optimised).
Control Risk & residual risk
Control Risk: risk introduced by implementation of countermeasures. Residual Risk (management chooses to accept) = Total Risk – Controls Gap. Management must formally document and SIGN the acceptance of residual risk. Reject/Ignore = negligence in court — NEVER acceptable.
Risk response strategies
Mitigation / Reduction
Apply a safeguard/control/countermeasure to reduce risk to an acceptable level.
Assignment / Transfer
Insurance or outsourcing — shift financial liability to a third party.
Deterrence
Auditing, cameras, banners, guards — discourage attacks before they occur.
Avoidance
Choose an alternate approach — fly instead of drive to eliminate the specific risk.
Acceptance
Cost of safeguard outweighs risk. Must be formally documented and signed. Becomes Residual Risk.
⚠ Reject / Ignore
Considered NEGLIGENCE in court. Never an acceptable strategy.

NIST CSF
Cybersecurity Framework — voluntary. Five functions: Identify → Protect → Detect → Respond → Recover. NIST 800-53 = security controls for Federal systems. NIST 800-171 = security controls for non-Federal systems. COBIT = examines effectiveness, efficiency, CIA, compliance, reliability of control objectives — heavy auditing, metrics, regulated industry.
ISO 27001 / 27005
ISO 27001: international standard for information security management systems (ISMS). ISO 27005: risk management framework. Baselines = minimum standards. ISO provides a consistent, measurable, standardised, comprehensive, modular approach.
COSO
Committee of Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission. Framework to work with Sarbanes-Oxley Section 404 compliance. Focuses on internal controls for financial reporting. Five components: Control environment, Risk assessment, Control activities, Information/communication, Monitoring.

Control types — tap to expand
Preventive
Deployed to thwart/stop unwanted activity BEFORE it occurs. Removes/reduces a vulnerability. Examples: separation of duties, job rotation, DLP, pen testing, auditing. Safeguard = removes/reduces vulnerability and protects against threats.
Deterrent
Deployed to discourage security policy violations. Does NOT prevent but discourages. Examples: guards, cameras, warning banners, disciplinary policies.
Detective
Deployed to discover/detect unwanted activity AFTER it occurs. Examples: guards, job rotation, mandatory vacations, audit trails, IDS/IPS, SIEM. Clipping levels = predefined thresholds before activity considered suspicious.
Compensating
Alternative control when primary is not feasible. Provides alternate means to satisfy the requirement. Examples: DRP, backup systems, alternate access paths.
Corrective
Modifies environment to RETURN system to normal after an incident. Examples: backup and restore, reboot, antivirus quarantine, patching.
Recovery
Extension of Corrective with more advanced/complex abilities. Examples: fault-tolerant drives, hot/warm/cold sites, RAID, full system redundancy.
Directive
Security policies, escape routes, loose attempt monitoring, supervision, guidance from security guard. Directs people toward compliant behaviours.
SCA — Security Control Assessment
If a security control's benefit CANNOT be quantified, evaluated, or compared — it DOES NOT actually provide security. SCA is generally implemented by FEDERAL AGENCIES based on NIST 800-53.
SCA Goal 1
Ensure the effectiveness of security mechanisms deployed in the environment.
SCA Goal 2
Evaluate the quality and thoroughness of the Risk Management (RM) process employed.
SCA Goal 3
Produce a report of the relative strength and weaknesses of the deployed security infrastructure. RM focuses on achieving business objectives — RM operations are integrated into business processes.
Security governance principles
Governance = who is accountable and how decisions are made. Senior management must drive the security programme (business proposal, positive ROI). Security is EVERYONE's responsibility but accountability rests with senior management.
Due care vs due diligence
Due Care: doing something about the threats — implementing countermeasures/controls. Failing to perform periodic security audits = perception due care is not maintained. Due Diligence: understanding the threats — properly investigating all weaknesses and vulnerabilities. Key: Due Diligence THEN Due Care.
Security policies hierarchy
Policy (highest) → Standards → Baselines → Guidelines (lowest — discretionary) → Procedures (step-by-step). Policies: mandatory, approved by management, high-level. Standards: mandatory rules implementing policy. Baselines: minimum security levels. Guidelines: recommendations. Procedures: detailed steps.
CISO / ISO role
ISO responsibilities: ensure written products are done, implement/operate CIRT, provide leadership for security awareness, communicate risk to higher management, report to as high a level as possible. CISO enforces administrative controls and compliance/policy requirements.
SOX — Sarbanes-Oxley 2002
Created after ENRON scandal. Section 302: CEO/CFOs can be sent to jail when signed information is incorrect. Section 404: about internal controls assessment — logical controls over accounting files. Corporate Officer Liability: executives held liable if organisation is non-compliant. COSO framework for SOX 404 compliance.
Intellectual property
Patent: grants ownership of invention — 20 years from filing date. Copyright: protects expression of ideas (poem, song, code) — 70 years after author's death. Trade Secret: proprietary company info (Coke formula) — DON'T REGISTER, no time limit. Trademark: words, names, symbols — 10 years renewable. Wassenaar Arrangement: international cryptographic export control.
Data classification governance
Classification criteria: value, age, usefulness, damage if disclosed. Government: Top Secret → Secret → Confidential → Unclassified. Commercial: Confidential → Private → Sensitive → Public. Data Owner classifies. Data Custodian implements controls. Regular review and declassification required.
Personnel security lifecycle
People are the WEAKEST LINK in the security chain — but the KEY security asset if trained and motivated. Every security programme must account for human behaviour.
Hiring process
Create JD/PD → Job classification → Background checks + social media footprint → Standardised interview (legally defensible) → Onboarding: NDA, AUP, Employment Agreements. IAM: grant account based on Principle of Least Privilege. Regular review and audit of JD required.
Mandatory vacations
Peer uses the SAME ACCOUNT of person on vacation — peer review process. Common in financial industry. Purpose: detect abuse, fraud, or negligence. Forces dependency exposure. Minimum one week. Most effective when surprise element maintained.
Separation of duties
No single person controls a critical process end-to-end. Creates checks and balances. Prevents fraud and errors. Two-man control: two people must review and approve. Dual control: two people required to complete a task. Both counter COLLUSION.
Job rotation & cross-training
Job rotation: employees rotate roles — limits time in any single security-sensitive position. Detects collusion. Builds cross-coverage. Cross-training: knowledge redundancy across staff — ensures continuity if key person leaves.
UBA / UEBA
User Behaviour Analytics / User and Entity Behaviour Analysis. Analyses behaviour of users, subjects, visitors, customers for specific security goals. UEBA expands to non-human entities (devices, servers). Used for insider threat detection.
Offboarding / termination
FIRST: disable the account. THEN: delete it after an appropriate interval. Exit interview conducted. Return all company assets. Remind departing employee of NDA obligations. Never delete immediately — may need forensic access later.
Third-party risk
Increased vulnerability/attack surface. Multiparty risk = expanded scope. VMS (Vendor Management System). SLAs with penalties. Risk management governing body to oversee multiparty projects. PII compliance obligations. Review before onboarding = due diligence.

Attack types
Phishing
Deceptive email stealing credentials or installing malware
Spear Phishing
Phishing crafted to a specific group or individual. Prepending: RE: FW: added to fool filters.
Whaling / BEC
Targets CEO/C-level execs. Attacker does significant research. CEO Fraud = BEC (Business Email Compromise).
Vishing
Voice-based phishing. SpIT = Spam over Internet Telephony.
Smishing
SMS phishing. SPIM = Spam over Instant Messaging.
Tailgating vs Piggybacking
Tailgating: access WITHOUT victim's knowledge. Piggybacking: victim TRICKED into providing consent.
Dumpster Diving
Gathering intelligence from discarded materials
Shoulder surfing
Visually observing credentials. Defence: screen filters, field masking, vigilance.
Baiting
Drops infected USB drives hoping someone picks them up
Typosquatting
Mistyped URL redirects to malicious site. URL Hijacking / Clickjacking.
Doxing
Collects and discloses info publicly to damage target's reputation
Influence campaigns
Hybrid warfare — manipulates public opinion via social media. Nonlinear warfare.
Invoice scams
False invoices with strong inducements to pay. Often combined with BEC.
Hoax
Proclaims imminent threat spreading across the internet requiring action
Email countermeasures & awareness
SPF / DKIM / DMARC
SPF: Sender Policy Framework — verifies sending server is authorised. DKIM: DomainKeys Identified Mail — cryptographic signature verification. DMARC: Domain Message Authentication Reporting and Conformance — filters spoofed messages, generates reports. All three together are essential anti-spoofing controls.
Awareness training hierarchy
1. Awareness: brings security to forefront — establishes common baseline. Must be fresh, creative, updated. 2. Training: targeted to groups with similar job functions. Tied with IRP, BCP, DRP. 3. Education: students learn MUCH MORE than strictly needed. Security Champions: non-security employees who encourage peers. Gamification: compliance through game play.
ISC² Code of Ethics — 4 canons
ISC² Code of Ethics does NOT refer to control. Strict adherence is a condition of certification. Preamble: safety and welfare of society requires adherence to highest ethical standards. When resolving conflicts: public safety → duties to principals → individuals → profession (in that order).
Canon 1
Protect Society, the Common Good, necessary public trust and confidence, and the Infrastructure. Promote/preserve public trust. Promote understanding of prudent security measures. Preserve integrity of public infrastructure. DISCOURAGE UNSAFE PRACTICES.
Canon 2
Act honorably, honestly, justly, responsibly and legally. Tell the truth — make all stakeholders aware of your actions on a timely basis. Observe all contracts and agreements. Give prudent advice — avoid raising unnecessary alarm or unwarranted comfort.
Canon 3
Provide diligent and competent service to principals. Preserve the value of their systems. Respect their trust and privileges. Avoid conflicts of interest. Render only services for which you are fully competent and qualified.
Canon 4
Advance and protect the profession. Sponsor those best qualified for advancement. Maintain competence — keep skills current. Give generously of your time and knowledge in training others. Avoid professional association with those whose practices might diminish the profession.
RFC 1087 — IAB (Jan 1989)
Defines unethical internet behaviour: 1. Unauthorised access 2. Misuse/unintended use of internet 3. WASTES resources (exam favourite!) 4. Destroys integrity 5. Compromises privacy. IAB considers wasting resources as unethical — even if you have access.
US cyber laws chronology
1984 — CCCA
Comprehensive Crime Control Act
First federal computer crime law.
1986 — CFAA
Computer Fraud and Abuse Act
Covers Federal Interest Computers. Outlawed malicious code. $5K damage threshold.
1991 — Federal Sentencing Guidelines
Prudent man rule
Minimise punishment by demonstrating due diligence. 3 burdens of proof for negligence.
1994 — CAAA
Computer Abuse Amendments Act
Broadened CFAA to international commerce. Imprisonment regardless of intent.
1996 — NIIPA
National Information Infrastructure Protection Act
Extended protections to railroads, gas pipelines, power grids, telecom circuits.
2002 — FISMA
Federal Information Security Management Act
NIST standards mandated for federal systems.
2014 — CEA / NCPA / FISMA 2014
Cybersecurity Enhancement + FISMA Modernisation
DHS integration centre. NIST CSF (voluntary). NIST 800-53 federal; NIST 800-171 non-federal.
Privacy laws chronology
1791 — 4th Amendment
"Every man's house is his castle"
Constitutional foundation of US privacy rights.
1974 — Privacy Act
Federal data disclosure law
FED's ability to disclose information. Written consent required. Formal access process.
1986 — ECPA
Electronic Communications Privacy Act
Crime to invade electronic privacy. $500 and 5 years prison for mobile wiretapping.
1996 — HIPAA
Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act
Protects health information for insurance and health maintenance organisations.
1998 — COPPA / ITADA
Children's Online Privacy + Identity Theft Act
Parental consent for under-13s. Identity theft = crime. Up to 15 years / $250K fine.
1999 — GLBA
Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act
Relaxed barriers between banks, insurance, credit providers.
2018 / 2020 — GDPR / CCPA
EU GDPR + California Consumer Privacy Act
GDPR lawful bases: Consent, Contract, Legal obligation, Vital interest, Legitimate interest. Privacy Shield invalidated 2020 (Schrems II). Standard Contractual Clauses replace.
